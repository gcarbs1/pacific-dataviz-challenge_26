I

The new geography of rain

Average annual rainfall anomaly in 2020–2025 minus the average of 1979–2000. The pattern is not random: the far western Pacific — Palau, Micronesia, the Solomon Islands, Vanuatu — is filling up, while a corridor of atoll nations along the equator and the dateline — Nauru, Kiribati, Tuvalu, Tokelau — and New Caledonia in the south are drying out. The rain has not vanished. It has moved.
II

Two eras, one tilt

Each thread is one territory, flowing from its average rainfall anomaly in 1979–2000 to its average in 2020–2025. Thicker threads moved further. Almost every thread crosses the old balance line — the water is being poured from one side of the ocean to the other.
III

Island by island, year by year

Forty-seven years of annual rainfall anomalies (vertical bars, shared scale) for the nine territories where the shift is sharpest. The white tick marks the 1979–2000 mean; the coloured tick, the 2020–2025 mean.
The islands the rain found
Average shift +15.4 mm — more water, but also more floods, landslides and crop rot.
Why the tilt points west
A warming Pacific concentrates convection over the western warm pool, where the ocean surface is hottest. Strengthened trade winds — amplified by the 2020–2023 “triple-dip” La Niña — drag moisture away from the central atolls and pile it onto Melanesia and the far west. The water that no longer falls on Nauru’s tanks falls instead on Vanuatu’s rivers.
The islands the rain left
Average shift −14.5 mm — on atolls with no rivers, every missing millimetre is drinking water.

The full picture

All 22 Pacific territories in the dataset, ordered from the largest gain to the deepest loss.

Data. SPC Pacific Data Hub — Dataflow DF_CLIMATE_CHANGE, indicator RAIN_ANOM (annual rainfall anomalies, mm, relative to the series baseline), 22 Pacific Island countries and territories, 1979–2025.
Method. Each territory’s shift is the difference between its mean anomaly in 2020–2025 and in 1979–2000. The recent window includes the 2020–2023 “triple-dip” La Niña, which amplifies — but does not create — the west-wet / centre-dry contrast.
